# Garment template manifest (F07 #142)

A canonical document pins `template: { id, version }`. `src/lib/template-manifest.ts` defines what that means, and `resolveTemplate` turns it into a manifest or an explicit error (`unknown-template` or `unsupported-version`). Saved designs are never quietly remapped onto different geometry.

## `tee-crew-short@2` (current) and `@1`

New designs pin `@2`. `@2` differs from `@1` only in how the back of the body unwraps (R02 #165, below); geometry, atlas, material, fonts and every other region are the same.

| Part | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Geometry | `public/models/tee.glb`, SHA-256 `50654ee6…8bac`, generated by `scripts/build-tee.mjs` (`createTeeMesh`) |
| Fixed structure | Crew neck, long hem, short sleeve. No other silhouette, drape or seam wrap is supported. |
| Atlas | 512 × 512 |
| Material | `#f3efe6`, roughness 0.86, metalness 0 |
| Fonts | Cormorant Garamond (`display`) and Outfit (`sans`), both OFL-1.1 |

| Canonical panel | Mesh | UV rectangle | Render panel (v1) | Axes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `front` | `body` | u 0–0.5, v 0.28–1 | `front` | x toward the wearer's left; y from hem to neckline |
| `back` | `body` | u 0.5–1, v 0.28–1 | `back` | x toward the wearer's left, like the front. The render panel is **mirrored** (`renderMirrorX`), so the 2D back canvas shows the back as seen from behind |
| `left` (wearer's left) | `body-sleeve-right` (+x) | u 0.5–1, v 0–0.28 | `right` | Around and along the sleeve |
| `right` (wearer's right) | `body-sleeve-left` (−x) | u 0–0.5, v 0–0.28 | `left` | Around and along the sleeve |

The front faces +z, so +x is the wearer's left. The tests rebuild the geometry and check every value in these tables: sleeve sides, chest direction, back direction, UV bounds, seam split, landmark heights and the collar overlap.

Landmarks (panel coordinates) are in the manifest. The torso heights come from the body rings: waist 0.321, chest 0.691, shoulder line 0.889.

### Back orientation (R02 #165): `@1` → `@2`

On `@1` geometry the back unwrapped u 0.5 → 1 from the wearer's right to the wearer's left, the same direction as the front. Seen from behind, screen-right is −x, so text drawn left-to-right on the 2D back canvas read mirrored on the garment. A browser pass confirmed it.

`@2` fixes this in the UV layout (`uvLayout: 2` in `createClothingMesh`):

- The back unwraps **u 1 → 0.5** from the wearer's right to the wearer's left. The back's atlas rectangle, and the 2D back canvas, now read left-to-right from behind.
- The wearer's right side seam (θ = π) is split into two columns, so the front ends at u 0 and the back starts at u 1. On `@1`, one column of triangles there stretched across the whole front row.
- **Canonical coordinates are unchanged and wearer-oriented.** Back `x` still runs toward the wearer's left, and the landmarks are identical. The region sets `renderMirrorX: true`, and `toRenderDocument` maps the back as render `x = 1 − x` and `rotation = −rotation` (stroke points too). The editor maps render-space input back with the same involution (`mirrorToRender`).

**Existing documents.** The scene always builds the newest layout. `render-projection.ts` reads each document's own template version, so stored content never changes:

| Pinned | Back projection | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `@2` (new designs) | mirrored | Canonical wearer's-left marks land on the wearer's left and read correctly from behind |
| `@1` (saved before #165, and every legacy import) | as stored | The 2D back canvas looks exactly as its author saw it, and now reads correctly from behind. Relative to the old mirrored render, back marks sit on the opposite side. |

Legacy v1 imports (`legacy-import.ts`) still pin `@1`, because v1 placement is render space and `@1` keeps it as stored.

**Front and sleeves.** These were checked and are not mirrored. `panel-orientation.test.ts` builds the mesh and checks every front, back and sleeve triangle: atlas +u × +v must face out of the garment, which means the panel reads correctly from outside. It also passes asymmetric text and strokes through the projection for both versions. The sleeve tubes keep three.js's cylinder UVs, where u runs around the sleeve and v from cuff to shoulder, and those read correctly from outside.

### Other findings

- **Collar (R02 #165).** The collar band's UVs overlap the front panel above y ≈ 0.861. The collar is trim, so it never takes the print: `buildCollar` marks the mesh `userData.printable = false` and `bindInkMap` leaves it on the base fabric (base colour, roughness and fabric map). A mark at the top of the front shows on the body only (`collar-print.test.ts`). No UVs changed, so the front, back and sleeve prints are where they were, the GLB hash is unchanged, and this needs no new template version. Both `@1` and `@2` designs render this way, because the scene builds one geometry for both.
- **Sleeve names.** The render panel `left` is built at −x, which is the wearer's right. The studio labels sleeves by wearer side (E02 #153).
- **Panel clipping.** `rasterizeLayers` clips every tee stroke, text, image and pattern stamp to its own panel's atlas rectangle. A mark near an edge therefore never lands on the other sleeve or on the torso (E02 #153). Concept garments are not clipped.

## Capture and framing (R04 #167)

A real-model design (`modelFrame` on its render document) is framed by the model itself. The builder records each model's bounds and each panel's centre in its manifest. `modelSeat` (`studio-camera.ts`) seats the front, back and three-quarter views in the tee's directions, at a distance that fits the model with room around it. It aims each sleeve view at the sleeve on that side, and a sleeveless garment gets a side view instead. Live views and published stills use the same seats. Everything below describes the procedural tee (`@1`–`@3`).

### The procedural tee

Studio views and published stills share one camera setup, `src/lib/studio-camera.ts`. The scene (`StudioStage.tsx`) and the capture path (`capture-still.ts`) both read it. The camera is separate from canonical content: switching views never changes the document or its revision.

| Part | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| Camera | Perspective, vertical fov 36°, near 0.1, far 2000. Every preset looks at `(0, 0.85, 0)`, the tee's centre, except the sleeve views, which look at their sleeve: `left` at (−0.26, 1.12, −0.02), `right` at (0.26, 1.12, −0.02) (`presetTarget`). |
| Presets | `front` (0, 1.18, 2.17), `threeQuarter` (1.22, 1.24, 1.79), `back` (0.08, 1.18, −2.14), and the sleeve views `left` (−0.85, 1.44, −1.07) and `right` (0.85, 1.44, −1.07). Like the render panels, `left` frames the sleeve at −x, the wearer's right. The sleeves stand out almost level and each sleeve panel's centre faces backwards, so the sleeve seats sit behind, outside and a little above their sleeve. A side-on seat on ±x (the first version, #263) saw only the cuff end-on. `orbit` is free orbit (distance 1.7 to 6.5, polar 45° to 88°) and resets to `threeQuarter`. Anything taller than the tee alone (the try-on mannequin, the landing stage, concept garments) uses `figure` framing: `front` (0, 1.48, 4.1), `threeQuarter` (2.35, 1.52, 3.45), `back` (0.15, 1.48, −4.05). |
| Published stills | Five, in order: `front`, `threeQuarter`, `back`, `left` (wearer's right), `right` (wearer's left) (`PUBLISHED_STILL_PRESETS`, `captureAngleStills`), uploaded as `view_front`, `view_three_quarter`, `view_back`, `view_left`, `view_right` (#179). The public page labels the sleeves "Wearer's right" and "Wearer's left". Publications made with the first three only stay public. The local look keeps three. Orbit, auto-rotate and the intro are off while capturing, and each still waits for the preset to settle. |
| Size and aspect | 480 × 600 (4:5), centre-cropped from the live canvas to cover the frame (`frameStill`) |
| Canvas shape | A canvas wider than 4:5 is cropped at the sides at fov 36°. A narrower (portrait) canvas would be cropped top and bottom, so its fov widens (`captureFov`), live and during capture, until the crop spans 36° vertically. Every canvas shape gives the same still. |
| Margins | Front, three-quarter and back: the whole garment, every vertex of the tee flat or worn, lies inside the still with at least 12% of the still's width or height clear on each side (`STILL_FRAME.minMargin`). Sleeve views: that whole sleeve, flat or worn, lies inside with the same margin and spans more than a quarter of the still; the body may run off the edge. |
| Background | Opaque, never transparent. The pixels are the scene background of the current environment, a top-to-bottom gradient: studio `#e6e5e2` to `#cbcac6` (the default) or outdoor `#87a4b8` to `#c4d2d6`. The frame fill, `#d9d8d4`, only shows if the canvas fails to cover the frame. Rendering: sRGB output, ACES filmic tone mapping, environment exposure, shadows on, and on the tee a cloth normal map (`fabric-normal.ts`) that changes shading only: no geometry, UV or atlas change, so no new template version. |

`capture-framing.test.ts` builds the tee (flat and worn), projects every vertex through each preset at canvas aspects 0.46 to 2.4, applies the same crop, and checks the margins and that every canvas shape gives the same still. For each sleeve view it also checks that the centre of that sleeve's print panel faces the camera (normal · view > 0.6), sits near the middle of the still and is not hidden by the rest of the garment (a raycast). Not covered: the try-on mannequin preview, which places the garment by avatar measurements.

## Versioning rule

- Any change to geometry, UVs or structure means **a new version**, plus a new archive entry. The GLB hash test fails until the version is bumped.
- Old versions stay in `TEMPLATE_ARCHIVE` for good, so saved designs keep opening. The scene builds only the newest geometry, and each archived version says how its panels project onto that geometry (`renderMirrorX`), so a design's stored content is never rewritten.
- A design pinned to a missing version shows the `unsupported-version` message; it is never silently opened on newer geometry.

### Image proportions: `@2` → `@3`

The atlas's rectangles don't match the garment. A square in the atlas measured 0.71:1 across the whole front and 1.70:1 on a sleeve, and every 2D panel was a further 6:7. So an uploaded square came out about 1.24:1 on the panel and 0.74:1 on the tee.

`@3` keeps the geometry, UVs and canonical coordinates, and adds a `surface` to each region, measured from the mesh:

| Region | `stretchY` | True panel shape (width / height) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front | 0.879 | 0.611 |
| Back | 0.871 | 0.605 |
| Sleeves | 1.701 | 3.037 |

- **Images and words** are drawn with `stretchY` applied after their turn, so they keep their shape at any angle. `scale` still means the same width in atlas units.
- **The 2D panels** take the true shape, so what you see while editing matches the cloth. The selection frame and hit areas follow what's drawn.
- **A new image** starts small enough to fit, because a sleeve is short.

**Existing documents.** A design pinned to `@1` or `@2` has no `surface`, so it draws exactly as before, with 6:7 panels. Only new designs pin `@3`. Published pictures are stored images and don't change.

**Limits.** The torso is an oval, so the cloth runs wider at its middle than its sides. The front and back numbers hold across the central half, where a print sits, and a mark towards a side seam reads narrower. Brush dots and base patterns still draw in atlas proportions: a dab is slightly oval on the cloth, and a check's cells aren't square. Making them true to the cloth changes what a local draft restore draws, because a draft re-imports on `@1`, so that stays a limit for the alpha.

## Template-built garments (real models)

A downloaded garment becomes a template with `scripts/build-template.mjs` and a hand-written piece map (`templates/<id>.pieces.json`). The tee is built by code, but these garments are pattern pieces sewn in a tool such as Style3D, and their own UVs drive the fabric texture. Printing uses a second UV set instead.

- **Panels:** each panel is a view (front, back, or the wearer's left or right) plus the mesh nodes, or their largest connected piece, that print on it. Run `--inspect <glb>` to list a model's nodes, pieces and bounds before writing the map.
- **Print UVs (`TEXCOORD_1`):**
  - A triangle prints when it belongs to a panel and faces that panel's viewer. Its print UV is a planar projection from that view, so marks read the right way round from where you look at them.
  - The inner layer of a two-sided shell faces away and never prints.
  - Every other vertex gets (−1, −1), which the ink shader (`apply-ink.ts`) leaves alone. Vertices shared between printed and unprinted triangles are split, so no ink smears across the atlas.
- **Atlas:** panels are packed into a 2048² print atlas at one shared scale (the hoodie gets about 17 px/cm). A mark therefore prints at the same physical size on every panel, and each panel's `surface` is its true shape (`stretchY` 1).
- **Manifest:** the builder writes `src/lib/templates/<id>.ts`, which pins the GLB's sha256. Register the manifest in `TEMPLATE_ARCHIVE` and `CURRENT_VERSIONS`. Any rebuild that changes the GLB needs a new version.
- **Runtime:** canonical panel ids equal the render panel ids, so there is no mirroring or sleeve swap. `toRenderDocument` hands the rectangles and atlas size to the renderer (`panelUv`, `atlasSize`), and the painter, picking and pointer code read them.

| Template | Panels | GLB |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hoodie@1` | front, back, left/right sleeve, hood left/right | `public/models/hoodie.glb` (`templates/hoodie.pieces.json`) |
| `tee-crew-short@4` | front, back, left/right sleeve (render routing kept: wearer's left on render `right`) | `public/models/tee-real.glb` (`templates/tee.pieces.json`); new tee designs pin it, and `@1`–`@3` designs keep the procedural tee |
| `mixed@1`, `coat@1`, `suit@1`, `tailored@1` | front, back, left/right sleeve | `public/models/<id>.glb` (`templates/<id>.pieces.json`) |
| `slip@1`, `overall@1`, `gown@1` | front, back | `public/models/slip.glb`, `overall.glb`, `garment-print.glb` |

Every garment but the hidden house jacket now has a template; front and back take all of a garment's cloth pieces (the facing test keeps them apart), and sleeves their own nodes. A board look on one of these garments opens in the studio with its cloth colour; its marks and art stay on the original.
