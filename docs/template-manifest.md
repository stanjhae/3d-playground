# Garment template manifest (F07 #142)

A canonical document pins `template: { id, version }`. `src/lib/template-manifest.ts` defines what that means, and `resolveTemplate` turns it into a manifest or an explicit error (`unknown-template` or `unsupported-version`). Saved designs are never quietly remapped onto different geometry.

## `tee-crew-short@2` (current) and `@1`

New designs pin `@2`. `@2` differs from `@1` only in how the back of the body unwraps (R02 #165, below); geometry, atlas, material, fonts and every other region are the same.

| Part | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Geometry | `public/models/tee.glb`, SHA-256 `50654ee6…8bac`, generated by `scripts/build-tee.mjs` (`createTeeMesh`) |
| Fixed structure | Crew neck, long hem, short sleeve. No other silhouette, drape or seam wrap is supported. |
| Atlas | 512 × 512 |
| Material | `#f3efe6`, roughness 0.86, metalness 0 |
| Fonts | Cormorant Garamond (`display`) and Outfit (`sans`), both OFL-1.1 |

| Canonical panel | Mesh | UV rectangle | Render panel (v1) | Axes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `front` | `body` | u 0–0.5, v 0.28–1 | `front` | x toward the wearer's left; y from hem to neckline |
| `back` | `body` | u 0.5–1, v 0.28–1 | `back` | x toward the wearer's left, like the front. The render panel is **mirrored** (`renderMirrorX`), so the 2D back canvas shows the back as seen from behind |
| `left` (wearer's left) | `body-sleeve-right` (+x) | u 0.5–1, v 0–0.28 | `right` | Around and along the sleeve |

The front faces +z, so +x is the wearer's left. The tests rebuild the geometry and check every value in these tables: sleeve sides, chest direction, back direction, UV bounds, seam split, landmark heights and the collar overlap.

Landmarks (panel coordinates) are in the manifest. The torso heights come from the body rings: waist 0.321, chest 0.691, shoulder line 0.889.

### Back orientation (R02 #165): `@1` → `@2`

On `@1` geometry the back unwrapped u 0.5 → 1 from the wearer's right to the wearer's left, the same direction as the front. Seen from behind, screen-right is −x, so text drawn left-to-right on the 2D back canvas read mirrored on the garment. A browser pass confirmed it.

`@2` fixes this in the UV layout (`uvLayout: 2` in `createClothingMesh`):

- The back unwraps **u 1 → 0.5** from the wearer's right to the wearer's left. The back's atlas rectangle, and the 2D back canvas, now read left-to-right from behind.
- The wearer's right side seam (θ = π) is split into two columns, so the front ends at u 0 and the back starts at u 1. On `@1`, one column of triangles there stretched across the whole front row.
- **Canonical coordinates are unchanged and wearer-oriented.** Back `x` still runs toward the wearer's left, and the landmarks are identical. The region sets `renderMirrorX: true`, and `toRenderDocument` maps the back as render `x = 1 − x` and `rotation = −rotation` (stroke points too). The editor maps render-space input back with the same involution (`mirrorToRender`).

**Existing documents.** The scene always builds the newest layout. `render-projection.ts` reads each document's own template version, so stored content never changes:

| Pinned | Back projection | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `@2` (new designs) | mirrored | Canonical wearer's-left marks land on the wearer's left and read correctly from behind |
| `@1` (saved before #165, and every legacy import) | as stored | The 2D back canvas looks exactly as its author saw it, and now reads correctly from behind. Relative to the old mirrored render, back marks sit on the opposite side. |

Legacy v1 imports (`legacy-import.ts`) still pin `@1`, because v1 placement is render space and `@1` keeps it as stored.

**Front and sleeves.** These were checked and are not mirrored. `panel-orientation.test.ts` builds the mesh and checks every front, back and sleeve triangle: atlas +u × +v must face out of the garment, which means the panel reads correctly from outside. It also passes asymmetric text and strokes through the projection for both versions. The sleeve tubes keep three.js's cylinder UVs, where u runs around the sleeve and v from cuff to shoulder, and those read correctly from outside.

### Other findings

- **Collar (R02 #165).** The collar band's UVs overlap the front panel above y ≈ 0.861. The collar is trim, so it never takes the print: `buildCollar` marks the mesh `userData.printable = false` and `bindInkMap` leaves it on the base fabric (base colour, roughness and fabric map). A mark at the top of the front shows on the body only (`collar-print.test.ts`). No UVs changed, so the front, back and sleeve prints are where they were, the GLB hash is unchanged, and this needs no new template version. Both `@1` and `@2` designs render this way, because the scene builds one geometry for both.
- **Sleeve names.** The render panel `left` is built at −x, which is the wearer's right. The studio labels sleeves by wearer side (E02 #153).
- **Panel clipping.** `rasterizeLayers` clips every tee stroke, text, image and pattern stamp to its own panel's atlas rectangle. A mark near an edge therefore never lands on the other sleeve or on the torso (E02 #153). Concept garments are not clipped.

## Capture and framing (R04 #167)

A real-model design (`modelFrame` on its render document) is framed by the model itself. The builder records each model's bounds and each panel's centre in its manifest. `modelSeat` (`studio-camera.ts`) seats the front, back and three-quarter views in the tee's directions, at a distance that fits the model with room around it. It aims each sleeve view at the sleeve on that side, and a sleeveless garment gets a side view instead. Live views and published stills use the same seats. Everything below describes the procedural tee (`@1`–`@3`).

### The procedural tee

Studio views and published stills share one camera setup, `src/lib/studio-camera.ts`. The scene (`StudioStage.tsx`) and the capture path (`capture-still.ts`) both read it. The camera is separate from canonical content: switching views never changes the document or its revision.

| Part | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| Camera | Perspective, vertical fov 36°, near 0.1, far 2000. Every preset looks at `(0, 0.85, 0)`, the tee's centre, except the sleeve views, which look at their sleeve: `left` at (−0.26, 1.12, −0.02), `right` at (0.26, 1.12, −0.02) (`presetTarget`). |
| Presets | `front` (0, 1.18, 2.17), `threeQuarter` (1.22, 1.24, 1.79), `back` (0.08, 1.18, −2.14), and the sleeve views `left` (−0.85, 1.44, −1.07) and `right` (0.85, 1.44, −1.07). Like the render panels, `left` frames the sleeve at −x, the wearer's right. The sleeves stand out almost level and each sleeve panel's centre faces backwards, so the sleeve seats sit behind, outside and a little above their sleeve. A side-on seat on ±x (the first version, #263) saw only the cuff end-on. `orbit` is free orbit (distance 1.7 to 6.5, polar 45° to 88°) and resets to `threeQuarter`. | Size and aspect | 480 × 600 (4:5), centre-cropped from the live canvas to cover the frame (`frameStill`) |
| Canvas shape | A canvas wider than 4:5 is cropped at the sides at fov 36°. A narrower (portrait) canvas would be cropped top and bottom, so its fov widens (`captureFov`), live and during capture, until the crop spans 36° vertically. Every canvas shape gives the same still. |
| Margins | Front, three-quarter and back: the whole garment, every vertex of the tee flat or worn, lies inside the still with at least 12% of the still's width or height clear on each side (`STILL_FRAME.minMargin`). `capture-framing.test.ts` builds the tee (flat and worn), projects every vertex through each preset at canvas aspects 0.46 to 2.4, applies the same crop, and checks the margins and that every canvas shape gives the same still. For each sleeve view it also checks that the centre of that sleeve's print panel faces the camera (normal · view > 0.6), sits near the middle of the still and is not hidden by the rest of the garment (a raycast). Not covered: the try-on mannequin preview, which places the garment by avatar measurements.

## Versioning rule

- Any change to geometry, UVs or structure means **a new version**, plus a new archive entry. The GLB hash test fails until the version is bumped.
- Old versions stay in `TEMPLATE_ARCHIVE` for good, so saved designs keep opening. The scene builds only the newest geometry, and each archived version says how its panels project onto that geometry (`renderMirrorX`), so a design's stored content is never rewritten.
- A design pinned to a missing version shows the `unsupported-version` message; it is never silently opened on newer geometry.

### Image proportions: `@2` → `@3`

The atlas's rectangles don't match the garment. A square in the atlas measured 0.71:1 across the whole front and 1.70:1 on a sleeve, and every 2D panel was a further 6:7. So an uploaded square came out about 1.24:1 on the panel and 0.74:1 on the tee.

`@3` keeps the geometry, UVs and canonical coordinates, and adds a `surface` to each region, measured from the mesh:

| Region | `stretchY` | True panel shape (width / height) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front | 0.879 | 0.611 |
| Back | 0.871 | 0.605 |
| Sleeves | 1.701 | 3.037 |

- **Images and words** are drawn with `stretchY` applied after their turn, so they keep their shape at any angle. `scale` still means the same width in atlas units.
- **The 2D panels** take the true shape, so what you see while editing matches the cloth. The selection frame and hit areas follow what's drawn.
- **A new image** starts small enough to fit, because a sleeve is short.

**Existing documents.** A design pinned to `@1` or `@2` has no `surface`, so it draws exactly as before, with 6:7 panels. Only new designs pin `@3`. Published pictures are stored images and don't change.

**Limits.** The torso is an oval, so the cloth runs wider at its middle than its sides. The front and back numbers hold across the central half, where a print sits, and a mark towards a side seam reads narrower. Brush dots and base patterns still draw in atlas proportions: a dab is slightly oval on the cloth, and a check's cells aren't square. Making them true to the cloth changes what a local draft restore draws, because a draft re-imports on `@1`, so that stays a limit for the alpha.

## Template-built garments (real models)

A downloaded garment becomes a template with `scripts/build-template.mjs` and a hand-written piece map (`templates/<id>.pieces.json`). The tee is built by code, but these garments are pattern pieces sewn in a tool such as Style3D, and their own UVs drive the fabric texture. Printing uses a second UV set instead.

- **Panels:** each panel is a view (front, back, or the wearer's left or right) plus the mesh nodes, or their largest connected piece, that print on it. Run `--inspect <glb>` to list a model's nodes, pieces and bounds before writing the map.
- **Print UVs (`TEXCOORD_1`):**
  - A triangle prints when it belongs to a panel and faces that panel's viewer. Its print UV is a planar projection from that view, so marks read the right way round from where you look at them.
  - The inner layer of a two-sided shell faces away and never prints.
  - Every other vertex gets (−1, −1), which the ink shader (`apply-ink.ts`) leaves alone. Vertices shared between printed and unprinted triangles are split, so no ink smears across the atlas.
- **Atlas:** panels are packed into a 2048² print atlas at one shared scale (the hoodie gets about 17 px/cm). A mark therefore prints at the same physical size on every panel, and each panel's `surface` is its true shape (`stretchY` 1).
- **Manifest:** the builder writes `src/lib/templates/<id>.ts`, which pins the GLB's sha256. Register the manifest in `TEMPLATE_ARCHIVE` and `CURRENT_VERSIONS`. Any rebuild that changes the GLB needs a new version.
- **Sleeves route like the tee's.** Canonical ids name the wearer's side: `left` is the wearer's left sleeve, labelled "Left sleeve" as worn. Render ids name the camera's side, where render `left` is the wearer's right, on every garment. The builder sets this routing (`renderPanel`) from the panel id and refuses a piece map that sets its own, or a `left` or `right` sleeve seen from the other side. So the editor tabs, the sleeve camera presets and the published side views mean the same sleeve on every garment. `glb-templates.test.ts` checks the sides against each sleeve's measured centre.
- **Runtime:** only the back is mirrored (`renderMirrorX`). `toRenderDocument` hands the rectangles and atlas size to the renderer (`panelUv`, `atlasSize`, keyed by render panel), and the painter, picking and pointer code read them. The camera fits the model by its `bounds` and aims each sleeve view at that render panel's `center`.

| Template | Panels | GLB |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hoodie@1` | front, back, left/right sleeve, hood left/right | `public/models/hoodie.glb` (`templates/hoodie.pieces.json`) |
| `tee-crew-short@4` | front, back, left/right sleeve | `public/models/tee-real.glb` (`templates/tee.pieces.json`); new tee designs pin it, and `@1`–`@3` designs keep the procedural tee |
| `mixed@1`, `coat@1`, `suit@1`, `tailored@1` | front, back, left/right sleeve | `public/models/<id>.glb` (`templates/<id>.pieces.json`) |
| `slip@1`, `overall@1`, `gown@1` | front, back | `public/models/slip.glb`, `overall.glb`, `garment-print.glb` |
| `vest@1`, `dress@1`, `trousers@1` | front, back | `public/models/vest.glb`, `dress.glb`, `trousers.glb`, baked from their pattern recipes (see "Baked from a pattern") |
| `crop@1` | front, back, left/right sleeve | `public/models/crop.glb`, baked from its pattern recipe |

Every garment but the hidden house jacket now has a template (the four D1 garments are our own baked models, with no credit line); front and back take all of a garment's cloth pieces (the facing test keeps them apart), and sleeves their own nodes. A board look on one of these garments opens in the studio with its cloth colour; its marks and art stay on the original.

## `tee-crew-short@5`: sleeves printed all the way round (16 Nov)

`@4` printed each sleeve as a flat view from its side: only triangles facing that side took ink, so the underside and the front and back of a sleeve stayed blank whatever you filled (founder review, 16 Nov). `@5` is the same model (`public/models/tee-real-v5.glb`, built from `@4`'s GLB by `templates/tee-v5.pieces.json`) with `"wrap": true` on both sleeve panels:

- **How it maps.** `scripts/build-template.mjs` finds each sleeve's own axis from its vertices and unrolls the sleeve around it. The seam is on the inner side, against the body, so a fill covers it all round. Only the outer layer prints, so no ink lands inside the sleeve.
- **Same placement, wider panel.** Near the outer face it maps as the flat view did, so a mark in the middle of a sleeve panel stays where it was. The sleeve panels are now wider than they are long: 33.9 × 24.2 cm and 31.3 × 26.0 cm unrolled, against about 14 × 24 cm flat.
- **Versions.** New designs pin `@5`. Designs on `@4` keep their look; the template upgrade re-pins them with their placements unchanged. `@4`'s GLB is untouched (`glb-templates.test.ts`).
- **Pattern templates and shape.** The pattern template has a `@5` entry too, with the same pattern and wider sleeve frames. Shape v1 already applies to `@4` and later.

## `tee-crew-short@6`: the sides print, and stripes meet at the seams (17 Nov)

`@5` printed the front and back as flat views: a triangle turned more than about 78° from the view (`FACING_MIN` 0.2) took no print UVs, so the side seams, shoulder tops and underarms never printed, and a fill or stripe base stopped short of the seam (founder, 17 Nov: "gaps between the 2D design areas and the 3D garment"). `@6` is the same model (`public/models/tee-real-v6.glb`, built from `@4`'s GLB by `templates/tee-v6.pieces.json`):

- **Front and back wrap round the body** (`"wrap": "torso"`). `build-template.mjs` traces the torso's real outline in 2 cm bands (the outermost vertex in each of 180 angle bins, smoothed round and up the body) and measures the arc length along it from each panel's centre line. Within 55° of the centre line print x is the flat view, as on `@5`, so a square mark looks square from the front (`render-proof.spec.ts`: 134 × 154 px on the tee, 0.87); from there it blends into the arc length by 88°, so at the sides a centimetre of cloth is a centimetre of print instead of being squeezed to nothing. (A fitted ellipse, the first cut, was rounder than the tee's front and squeezed art across the chest to 0.81.) A triangle prints when it faces out from the body's axis (`"facingMin": 0.05`), so the sides, shoulder tops and underarms belong to the front or the back, meeting at the side seam. About 99.5% of the torso's triangles print, against 84% on `@5`; what is left is at the armhole and hem folds.
- **Sleeves** are wrapped as on `@5`, and `"closeSleeveSeam": true` unrolls the triangles the inner seam runs through past the far edge, so the sleeve has no unprinted strip there.
- **Wider padding** (`"padding": 16` px between packed panels, against 8), for the atlas bleed below.
- **Print-space frames.** Each region carries `frame: { x0, x1, y0, y1 }` in metres: canonical x toward the wearer's left, y up. The front's runs seam to seam; the back is given the same span (its own arc is about 4% shorter), so a pattern phased from the origin meets itself at both side seams. `toRenderDocument` passes the frame to the base stripe and check layers (`garmentPhase`), which then cover the whole panel phased from that origin instead of each panel's own corner. Templates without frames draw as before.
- **Panels.** 56.4 × 69.4 cm (front) and 53.9 × 70.3 cm (back) unrolled, against 43.3 × 69.0 and 44.2 × 70.3 flat; the sleeves are 35.2 × 24.4 and 34.6 × 26.4 cm. At one shared scale the atlas holds 18.1 px per cm (21.0 on `@5`). The landing pad's shapes (`LANDING_PANEL_ASPECT`, `PAD_ASPECT_CLASS`) follow: front 0.813, back 0.761.
- **Versions.** New tee designs pin `@6`; designs on `@5` and earlier keep their look, and the template upgrade re-pins them with their placements unchanged (a mark keeps its panel coordinates, so on the wider panel it sits a little smaller). `@4`'s and `@5`'s GLBs are untouched (`glb-templates.test.ts`). The pattern template has a `@6` entry with the same recipe.
- **Comparison.** `scripts/seams-compare.html` prints a stripe base and a word near the wearer's left seam on both versions: `docs/evidence/seams-front.png`, `seams-three-quarter.png` and `seams-side.png`.

## `tee-crew-short@7`: both sleeves print all round, the same on each side (18 Nov)

On `@6` the wearer's right sleeve left 9.1% of its cloth unprinted against 4.7% on the left, in streaks along the underarm (founder, round 2), and the two flat sleeve templates were different shapes. `@7` is the same model (`public/models/tee-real-v7.glb`, built from `@4`'s GLB by `templates/tee-v7.pieces.json`); front and back are exactly `@6`'s.

- **Why the sleeves were bare.** `build-template.mjs` unrolled each sleeve about its "main direction", the principal axis of its vertices. A short sleeve is about as wide round as it is long, so that axis came out nearly vertical on the wearer's left and tilted the wrong way on the right (the model's right sleeve hangs lower and further back). Measured from that axis, the flanks of the underarm folds looked like the inside of the sleeve and took no print UVs, about twice as many on the right. The same axis pointed up the arm instead of down, so on `@5` and `@6` panel y ran from the shoulder to the cuff: a mark drawn upright on a flat sleeve showed upside down, and mirrored, on the 3D one (`glb-templates.test.ts` measures it).
- **Axis from the surface** (`"sleeveAxis": "normals"`). A tube's normals all lie across its axis, so the axis is the direction they leave out: the least eigenvector of their area-weighted covariance. Both sleeves now unroll about the line they really hang along, shoulder to cuff, and panel y runs up the arm as on the body.
- **Folds print** (`"sleeveFolds": true`). Each sleeve is one layer of cloth, wound one way (no edge shared by more than two triangles, none flipped, over 90% facing out); the bake checks that and stops otherwise. So every triangle of the sleeve is the outside of the cloth, a fold's inward-facing flank included, and all of it prints: 100% of each sleeve by area (`glb-templates.test.ts` asks for at least 97% each, within 1.5 points of each other).
- **Mirror images** (`"mirrorSleeves": true`). The right sleeve is unrolled in the left's handedness and flipped across, and the two share one box, so panel x = u on the right sleeve is the mirror of 1 − u on the left, the seam's overlap at the back of both. Both are 37.4 × 25.4 cm. In the studio the two traced outlines are averaged into one, the right's flipped across (`cleanSleevePair` in `src/lib/panel-shape.ts`), so both tabs and the flat templates show the same smooth sleeve.
- **Versions.** New tee designs pin `@7`. Designs on `@6` and earlier keep their look until their author takes the upgrade (`TemplateUpgradeBanner`). Between real-tee versions with print-space frames, `physicalMapping` maps each point through both frames, so a mark keeps its size and place on the cloth; from `@5` and `@6` a sleeve mark moves to where it sat on the cloth but keeps its shape as drawn, so it now reads the right way up. Front and back marks don't move. `@4`–`@6`'s GLBs are untouched (`glb-templates.test.ts`). The pattern template has a `@7` entry with the same recipe and the new sleeve frames.
- **Evidence.** `docs/evidence/sleeves-v7-left-right.png`: both sleeves filled, from each side.

### Atlas bleed (atlas compiler 2)

Each panel's ink is clipped to its rectangle, and the garment's print UVs reach its very rim, so texture filtering and the lower mip levels blended in the transparent padding round it: ink faded at a panel's edge and left a pale line along a seam. `rasterizeLayers` now:

- rounds clip edges **outward** (an edge that would reach a neighbouring rectangle, as on the procedural tee, rounds as before), so the rim texels the UVs reach are drawn;
- **bleeds** each panel's edge pixels into the padding round it, up to `bleedRadius` (`width / 128`: 16 px at 2048), visiting only that band. A pixel between two panels takes the nearer one's edge, so neighbours split the gap. The live stroke cache re-bleeds only the stroke's panel.

The procedural tee (`@1`–`@3`) has no padding, so it draws as before; template-built garments' atlases changed, so `ATLAS_COMPILER_VERSION` is 2 and the goldens were re-approved.

## Pattern templates (Workflow 1, pattern v1)

Each garment's current template version has a **pattern template**: the sewing pattern a design starts from in Pattern mode, plus a fixed print frame per panel (`src/lib/pattern/templates.ts`). A design records the one it used in `pattern.basis` (`docs/canonical-document.md`).

| Template | Pieces |
| --- | --- |
| `tee-crew-short@4` | Front, back, two set-in sleeves |
| `hoodie@1` | Front, back, two sleeves, two hood halves (`hood-left`, `hood-right`) |
| `coat@1`, `suit@1`, `tailored@1` | A deep-V front, back, two sleeves. The suit adds trousers: a front and a back across both legs |
| `mixed@1` | A shirt over a two-piece skirt |
| `slip@1`, `overall@1`, `gown@1` | Front and back: straps and a V, a square bib, or a fitted floor-length bodice |
| `vest@1` | A sleeveless front and back, cut deep under the arm, with a V front (woven) |
| `crop@1` | Front, back, two short sleeves; the hem about 80 mm above the waist (jersey) |
| `dress@1` | A bodice to the waist (woven), sewn there to a flared two-piece skirt (satin). The bodice's hem is the waist seam (role `waist`), so hem edits act on the skirt |
| `trousers@1` | A front and a back across both legs, full length, at the waist (woven). **Shorts** are a length preset of the same pattern (below) |

- **Built from families.** Templates are built from pattern families (`src/lib/pattern/families.ts`: bodice, set-in sleeve, hood, skirt, trousers), not from baked files. A shipped `patternVersion` must never change, so `templates.test.ts` pins each one by the hash of its pattern and of its frames. If a change to a family moves a pinned hash, give that garment a new `patternVersion` and keep the old one, as template versions are kept.
- **Frames.** Each panel's pieces are laid into one frame in millimetres: stacked top to bottom, centred, and sized to the GLB panel's aspect (`surface.panelAspect`). Panel-normalised prints keep their proportions on the cloth. A back panel's frame is mirrored, so it reads as seen through the body, as the back panel does today.
- **The drape body** is a dress form built from the mannequin's shapes (`src/lib/cloth/colliders.ts`):
  - The hips are wider than the waist, and the shoulders reach the arms.
  - Shoulders and hips hold cloth with high friction.
  - A skirt or trouser waistband can't sag more than 1.5 cm.
  - Upper garments worn over skirts or trousers sit 8 mm out.
- **Presets.** A recipe may offer other starting patterns on the same template (`presets` in `templates.ts`; `patternPresetsFor`, `patternPresetFor`). The trousers offer **Shorts**: the same pieces, point ids and seams, cut 420 mm from waist to hem, laid in the trousers' own frames with the waist where the trousers' waist is, so a print at the waist stays put. Pattern mode shows them as "Start from"; "Reset pattern" goes back to full length. A preset is only a pattern a design starts from: the design still pins `trousers@1`, and its stored pattern says it is shorts. `templates.test.ts` pins the preset's hash too.
- **Simplified for the beta.** Coats and suits have a one-piece deep-V front instead of overlapping lapels (spike S2): overlapping fronts would need cloth-to-cloth collision.

## Baked from a pattern (roadmap D0)

A garment that has only a pattern recipe gets its GLB and manifest from its own drape, with no downloaded model: `scripts/bake-pattern-glb.mjs` (`pnpm bake:pattern`).

```bash
pnpm bake:pattern --template tee-crew-short --version 5
```

**What it does:**

1. **Drape.** It runs `drape-job.ts` headlessly in node, with the same pure-TS solver as the studio's cloth worker, for the recipe's pattern (`patternTemplateFor`) on the neutral body. The drape stays in the mannequin's frame (floor at y = 0). It isn't seated on an older model.
2. **Atlas.** It packs the panels' pattern frames (millimetres) into a 2048² atlas at one shared scale (`scripts/atlas-pack.mjs`, the same packer `build-template.mjs` uses), in the tee's panel order. Each rectangle keeps its frame's shape.
3. **Scene.** It builds the scene with `buildPatternScene` (`pattern-scene.ts`, with `seat: [0, 0, 0]`). That gives one double-sided mesh, `body-pattern`, with fabric UVs in `uv` and print UVs in `uv1` from each piece's place in its frame. `uv1` becomes `TEXCOORD_1` when three's `GLTFExporter` writes the GLB.
4. **Slim steps.** `simplify-parts.mjs` welds (and simplifies with `--body-ratio`). Then `compress-models.sh <file>` applies Draco with `--prune-attributes false`, because no material samples the print UVs and `optimize` would otherwise drop them as unused.
5. **Manifest.** `build-template.mjs` runs on a generated spec with `"printUv": "keep"`. In that mode it doesn't project print UVs. Instead it checks that every printing vertex's `TEXCOORD_1` lies in [0, 1] inside one region, measures each panel's `center` and the `bounds`, and writes the manifest with the GLB's sha256. Regions come from the frames:
   - `uv` is the packed rectangle;
   - `surface.panelAspect` is the frame's width over its height;
   - `renderMirrorX` is the frame's `mirrorX` (the back only);
   - sleeves route like every other garment's (`renderPanel`, wearer-side labels).

**Output.** By default it writes `.bake/<id>@<version>/<id>-v<version>.glb` and `.ts`. That folder is git-ignored and never shipped. To ship a bake, pass `--glb public/models/<id>.glb --manifest src/lib/templates/<id>.ts`, plus `--garment <id>` for a template that isn't in the archive yet. Then register it as for any template-built garment (above).

**Deterministic.** The recipe, solver and pinned tools give the same bytes on every run, so a re-bake leaves the sha256 as it was. `src/lib/pattern/bake-pattern-glb.test.ts` checks this. It bakes the tee uncompressed, because the Draco step fetches the gltf-transform CLI. It also checks `TEXCOORD_1`, that regions lie in [0, 1] without overlapping, and the routing checks `glb-templates.test.ts` applies to shipped templates.

**Comparing.** Run `pnpm dev`, then open `/scripts/bake-compare.html?recipe=<id>@<version>`. This dev-only page shows the shipped model next to the bake, front and back, each printed with a test atlas from its own manifest. Each panel gets a colour, a letter, a grid and an up arrow. A panel in the wrong place, upside down or mirrored shows at once.

**The tee proof (16 Nov).** `tee-crew-short@5` baked from its recipe ([comparison](evidence/bake-tee-v5-compare.jpg)):

- **Size.** 1,537 vertices and 12.7 KiB with Draco, against the 1228.8 KiB `glbPerTemplateKiB` budget.
- **Mapping.** The front, back and both sleeves land where `tee-real-v5.glb` puts them, upright, with the back the only mirrored panel. The sleeve frames have the same aspect as `@5`'s wrapped sleeves.
- **Shape.** The drape is boxier than the Style3D model, and its sleeves stand further out: it measures 67 cm tall by 74 cm across, against the model's 71 by 57 cm. The sleeves print as pattern pieces rather than unrolled tubes.
- **Not shipped.** `@5` keeps `tee-real-v5.glb`. New garments (roadmap D1) are the first to ship baked GLBs.

## The D1 garments (17 Nov): vest, crop top, dress, trousers

The first garments shipped from their own drape, with no downloaded model:

```bash
pnpm bake:pattern --template vest --version 1 --garment vest --glb public/models/vest.glb --manifest src/lib/templates/vest.ts
```

Each is far inside the 1228.8 KiB `glbPerTemplateKiB` budget. `templates.test.ts` checks each settles where it is cut to (the crop's hem 50 to 110 mm above the waist, the dress's bodice meeting its skirt, the trousers at the waist and the ankle), and that the shorts preset hangs at the same waist. `glb-templates.test.ts` pins each GLB's sha256 and runs every check the real models get.

Five things changed along the way:

- **Atlas packing.** `atlas-pack.mjs` started its search at 1 px per unit, which suits boxes in metres. A dress's frames are over a metre and a half tall in millimetres, so two of them didn't fit at that scale and the packer gave up. It now starts lower when it has to; boxes that fit at 1 px per unit pack exactly as before, so no shipped template moves.
- **Flat sides with more than one piece.** The 2D panel's printable shape kept only the largest outline, so the dress's front showed its skirt and not its bodice. It now keeps every outline at least a tenth the size of the largest (`cleanPanelShape`).
- **No selection outline on open cloth.** The hover and selection outline is an inflated back-facing shell. On a garment draped from its pattern (one open layer of cloth, mesh `body-pattern`: these GLBs, or the live drape in Pattern mode), the shell showed through the neck, armholes and hem as a gold lining. Such a mesh gets no outline (`isOpenClothMesh`).
- **Pattern (beta) reaches every garment with a pattern.** The Garment step showed the Pattern (beta) switch only where shape sliders exist, which is the tee alone, so no other garment could open Pattern mode. It now shows wherever the build has Pattern (beta) on and the garment has a pattern template (`create.tsx`). With the beta off, nothing changes.
- **The garment picker follows a loading design.** Its category was set once, at first render, before a saved design's garment arrived, so a pair of trousers opened on "Tops". It now follows the open garment (`SilhouetteSwitch.tsx`).
